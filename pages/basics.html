# Laplace Transform Basics ## Definition The Laplace Transform of a function f(t), defined for t ≥ 0, is given by the integral: \[ F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st}f(t)dt \] where \( s \) is a complex number. ## Properties 1. **Linearity**: If \(a\) and \(b\) are constants, \( \mathcal{L}\{af(t) + bg(t)\} = aF(s) + bG(s) \) 2. **Differentiation**: \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = sF(s) - f(0) \) 3. **Integration**: \( \mathcal{L}\{\int_{0}^{t} f(\tau)d\tau\} = \frac{F(s)}{s} \) 4. **Time shifting**: If \( f(t) \) is shifted by \( a \), \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t - a)u(t - a)\} = e^{-as}F(s) \) 5. **Frequency shifting**: \( \mathcal{L}\{e^{at}f(t)\} = F(s - a) \) ## Convergence Region The integral defining the Laplace Transform converges for certain values of \( s \). The convergence region is determined by the growth of \( f(t) \). The Laplace Transform exists for \( s \) in the complex plane for which the integral converges, usually characterized by a vertical line in the s-plane. ## Inverse Laplace Transform The Inverse Laplace Transform is defined as: \[ f(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\} = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{c - i\infty}^{c + i\infty} e^{st}F(s)ds \] where \( c \) is a real number in the right half of the region of convergence of \( F(s) \). ### Techniques for Finding the Inverse 1. **Partial Fraction Expansion**: Allows breaking down complex fractions into simpler parts. 2. **Table of Transforms**: Utilizing known transforms and their inverses for quick referencing. 3. **Residue Theorem**: For finding coefficients in the expansion using contour integration.
